# Q1 2026 Revenue Forecast - Summary for the CEO

**We expect to sign $26.4M of new business in the first quarter of 2026** - $8.0M in
January, $9.1M in February and $9.4M in March. That is about +58% against the $16.8M we
signed in the same quarter last year, and there is roughly an 8-in-10 chance the true
figure lands between $16.6M and $38.8M. No single factor dominates: the forecast leans
in roughly equal measure on how far through the five years we are, revenue three months
ago and which calendar month it is (each around 19% of its predictive power). January is
reliably our weakest month of the year and April the strongest, and the fourth quarter
runs below the annual average, so the year-end months are not where the money is.

**How much should you trust it?** The forecast was tested by hiding the most recent
twelve months from the model and asking it to predict them blind. It came within 28% of
the real figure in a typical month, or about $1.9M. That is useful for planning and
direction-setting, but it is not precise enough to commit to a specific number - monthly
revenue in this business swings widely, and no model removes that. Treat the range as
the forecast and the single figure as its midpoint.

**Where the money comes from.** Climate and Carbon Markets remains the largest
contributor at $7.6M for the quarter (29% of the total), followed by Technology and
Innovation and Energy. Confidence varies a great deal by sector: the largest sectors are
the most predictable, while smaller ones can be swung by a single contract landing a few
weeks either side of a month end.

**Three recommendations.**

1. **Plan January conservatively.** It is reliably our weakest month of the year, and
   this forecast is no exception - we expect $8.0M, roughly 15% below March. Set cash
   and resourcing plans against the lower end of the range, not the middle.

2. **Resource the spring, not the year-end.** In this data the strong season is the
   second quarter - April is the peak month - while Q4 sits below the annual average. Q1
   is the run-up to that peak, so make sure delivery capacity and pipeline are in place
   to convert it, and do not over-invest in a year-end push that the numbers do not
   support.

3. **Tighten pipeline reporting where the forecast is weakest.** Public Sector and
   Finance and Insurance are the two sectors the model predicts least reliably - it
   missed them by 104% and 87% respectively in testing, against 44% for our most
   predictable sector. A weekly pipeline review in those two will improve the overall
   forecast more than the same effort spent anywhere else.

**The one assumption that matters.** This forecast assumes the next three months behave
broadly like the last five years - same seasonal rhythm, same growth trajectory, no
major shock. It cannot anticipate anything it has not seen before: losing a top client,
a change in the market, or an unusually large deal would all put the actual figure
outside the range above.
